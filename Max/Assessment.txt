You said

I have a 7th grader who really struggles with reading/writing, but more particularly spelling. His name is Max. He is going into seventh grade (we are just going into the summer after him finishing 6th grade). He does generally well in school but he has a terrible attitude, and we are looking into therapy options because we think he may be addicted to gaming. Now that they've (he has a brother going into 6th grade) had a few weeks off of school, I am wanting to do some educational exercises with him routinely over the summer, starting today with reading/writing. The plan is to do an hour and 15 minutes, with the structure being 30 minutes, 15 minute break, 30 minutes. I want to spend the first 30 minutes doing some kind of focused assessment on what his abilities are, and then after the break, begin some targeted work.

Here’s the clearest, most effective way to use that first 1 hour and 15 minutes with Max — in a way that gives you real diagnostic information and gives him structure, predictability, and a sense that this isn’t punishment, it’s skill‑building.

I’ll give you:

A 30‑minute assessment plan (zero prep, highly targeted)

A 15‑minute break structure

A 30‑minute targeted‑work block based on what you discover

What to watch for (so you know what his struggles actually mean)

⭐ 30‑Minute Assessment Block (Fast, Clear, No Stress)
This is NOT a test. It’s you gathering data.

Part 1 — Reading Fluency Check (8 minutes)
Goal: See how he decodes, handles multisyllabic words, and manages attention.

What to do:  
Give him a grade‑level paragraph (6th–7th grade nonfiction works best).
Ask him to read aloud for one minute.

You observe:

Does he guess at words?

Does he skip endings (‑ed, ‑ing, ‑s)?

Does he lose his place?

Does he sound choppy or monotone?

Does he self‑correct?

Then let him finish the paragraph silently and tell you what it was about in 2–3 sentences.

This gives you fluency + comprehension + working memory data in under 10 minutes.

Part 2 — Spelling Diagnostic (12 minutes)
This is the most important part for Max.

You dictate 12 words, each chosen to reveal a different pattern.
Have him write them on paper.

Use these:

action

decision

special

picture

bicycle

energy

running

complete

family

discover

visible

transport

Why these?  
They hit every major pattern 6th–7th graders struggle with:
‑tion, ‑sion, ‑cial, ‑ture, soft c/g, doubling rules, silent‑e, schwa vowels, prefixes, suffixes, Latin roots.

You observe:

Does he spell phonetically (e.g., “akshun”)?

Does he drop vowels?

Does he confuse soft c/g?

Does he over‑ or under‑double consonants?

Does he show any awareness of meaning (roots/prefixes)?

This gives you a map of where to target instruction.

Part 3 — Writing Sample (10 minutes)
Prompt:
“Write a short paragraph about your favorite game, show, or hobby. At least 5 sentences.”

You observe:

Sentence boundaries

Capitalization

Spelling in context

Organization

Attitude/avoidance behaviors

Whether he can sustain writing for 10 minutes

This tells you how his skills hold up in real writing, not isolated tasks.

⭐ 15‑Minute Break
This is important for a kid with gaming‑related dopamine patterns.

Rules for the break:

No screens

Snack + movement

Something sensory‑resetting (walk, trampoline, pet the dog, stretch)

Keep it predictable: “We start again at 10:15.”

⭐ 30‑Minute Targeted Work Block (Based on What You Saw)
Here’s how to use the second half:

Step 1 — Pick ONE pattern he struggled with (5 minutes)
Examples:

If he missed action/decision → work on ‑tion/‑sion

If he missed bicycle/energy → work on soft c/g

If he missed running → work on doubling rules

If he missed family/chocolate → work on schwa vowels

Don’t try to fix everything.
One pattern per day builds mastery and confidence.

Step 2 — Teach the Pattern (5 minutes)
Short, simple explanation.
Example:
“‑tion always sounds like ‘shun.’ You almost never spell that sound the way it sounds.”

Kids like Max need explicit rules, not “sound it out.”

Step 3 — Guided Practice (10 minutes)
Use 5 words from the pattern.
Break each word into:

syllables

chunks

meaning

spelling aloud

writing once slowly

This is the same structure we used in your earlier 14‑day plan — predictable and effective.

Step 4 — One Funny Paragraph (5 minutes)
You read it aloud.
He reads it aloud.
He finds the pattern words.
He laughs a little.
He forgets he’s “bad at spelling.”

I can generate these paragraphs daily.

Step 5 — One Sentence (3 minutes)
He writes a sentence using one of the words.

Step 6 — Confidence Close (2 minutes)
One specific praise:
“You broke the word into syllables before guessing — that’s real progress.”

⭐ What You’re Actually Diagnosing Today
You’ll know by the end of the first hour whether Max struggles with:

Phonological decoding (reading aloud is choppy)

Orthographic mapping (spelling patterns don’t stick)

Working memory (he forgets instructions mid‑task)

Executive function (attitude, avoidance, shutdowns)

Motivation/dopamine imbalance (common in gaming‑heavy kids)

Writing stamina (10 minutes feels impossible)

This helps you decide whether you need:

structured literacy

executive function supports

therapy for gaming addiction

or a combination
