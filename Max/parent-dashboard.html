✍️ Part 3: Writing Sample - What to Look For

He's Learning the Structure of a Complete Sentence:

A complete sentence has: (1) a subject (WHO/WHAT), (2) an action verb (WHAT they do), and (3) a complete thought (it makes sense on its own). Examples shown: "I play soccer" (complete) vs "Playing soccer" (incomplete—missing WHO).

What to Look For in His Writing:

Are all 5 sentences COMPLETE sentences (have subject + verb + complete thought)?
Capitalization at the start of sentences
Periods at the end of sentences
Spelling attempts (even if not perfect—look for patterns)
Organization (follows the 5 steps in order)
Attitude during writing (engaged? frustrated? avoidant?)
Writing stamina (can he sustain 10 minutes of writing?)

📋 Assessment Guide & Analysis

What You've Learned

By the end of the 1-hour assessment, you'll know whether Max struggles with:

1. Phonological Decoding
Reading aloud is choppy, hesitant, or error-prone. He guesses at words or skips word parts.
2. Orthographic Mapping
Spelling patterns don't stick. Errors are phonetic or show no awareness of common patterns.
3. Working Memory
He forgets instructions mid-task or loses his place frequently.
4. Executive Function
Attitude issues, avoidance behaviors, shutdown during writing, difficulty starting tasks.
5. Motivation/Dopamine Imbalance
Common in gaming-heavy kids. He needs external validation or gives up quickly.
6. Writing Stamina
10 minutes of writing feels impossible. He needs frequent breaks or refuses entirely.

Next Steps

Based on your observations, you'll decide whether Max needs:

  • Structured Literacy Instruction — If phonological decoding and orthographic mapping are weak
  • Executive Function Supports — If attitude, avoidance, or task initiation are issues
  • Dopamine/Motivation Intervention — If gaming addiction or motivation is the root cause
  • Combination Approach — Most likely scenario
