The Great Migration of Monarch Butterflies

Every fall, millions of monarch butterflies embark on one of nature's most remarkable journeys. These delicate insects migrate thousands of miles from Canada and the northern United States to the mountains of central Mexico. Scientists have long been fascinated by how monarchs accomplish this incredible feat without a map or GPS. The butterflies navigate using the sun's position and the Earth's magnetic field. Their journey is dangerous, and many monarchs don't survive. But those who make it to Mexico spend the winter in dense clusters on specific trees, waiting for spring. When spring arrives, the monarchs begin their journey north again, laying eggs along the way. These eggs will hatch into the next generation of butterflies that will continue the migration.
