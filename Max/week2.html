The Northern Lights

The Northern Lights, also called the Aurora Borealis, are one of Earth's most stunning natural displays. They light up the sky in the Arctic regions, particularly around countries like Norway, Canada, and Alaska. Scientists have studied this phenomenon for centuries, trying to understand its mysterious beauty. The lights are caused by a fascinating process involving the Sun and Earth's atmosphere. The Sun constantly releases charged particles called solar wind. When these particles reach Earth, they interact with our planet's magnetic field, which protects us like a shield. As the charged particles collide with gases in Earth's upper atmosphere—particularly oxygen and nitrogen—they create the colorful light show we see. The colors depend on which gas is hit. Oxygen produces green and red lights, while nitrogen creates blue and purple colors. The Aurora Borealis typically appears in a region called the auroral oval. This region forms a circle around the North Pole, usually between 65 and 72 degrees latitude. Places in northern Scandinavia, northern Canada, and Alaska experience frequent displays during winter months when the sky stays dark longer. Many people travel thousands of miles just to witness this extraordinary event. Indigenous peoples of the Arctic have observed and created legends about the Northern Lights for thousands of years. Today, viewing the Aurora Borealis has become an unforgettable experience for millions of visitors worldwide.

Part 2: Spelling Practice
Why we're learning this: This week we're focusing on two important spelling patterns that show up everywhere in 7th grade writing.

Rule 1 — Double Consonants: When a short vowel comes before a single consonant at the end of a word, you usually double that consonant before adding an ending. Example: "run" → "running", "stop" → "stopped". Words this week: hurry, summer, button, dinner, happen, success.

Rule 2 — Endings that sound different than they look: The endings -tion (sounds like "shun"), -ture (sounds like "cher"), and -sure (sounds like "zher") are very common in English. Words this week: station, question, creature, treasure, pleasure, measure.
