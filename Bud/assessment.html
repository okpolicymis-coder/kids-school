🧪 The Scientific Method

Let's learn how scientists think and solve problems!

Part 1: Understanding the Scientific Method

What You're Learning: The scientific method is how scientists ask questions and find answers. It's not magic—it's a step-by-step way of thinking about problems.

Why it matters: You use the scientific method every day without realizing it! When you figure out why something isn't working or test a new idea, you're thinking scientifically.

The Scientific Method: 5 Steps to Solve Any Problem

Step 1: Ask a Question "I wonder WHY...?" A good scientist always starts with a question about something they observe. Example: "Why does my plant grow faster in the window than in the dark?"
Step 2: Do Research Look up what others have already learned about your question. Read books, watch videos, or ask experts. You want to understand what's already known.
Step 3: Make a Hypothesis A hypothesis is an educated guess—NOT a random guess. Based on what you know, you predict what will happen. Example: "I think the plant will grow faster in the window BECAUSE it gets more sunlight."
Step 4: Do an Experiment Test your hypothesis. Change ONE thing (the variable) and measure what happens. Keep everything else the same. Example: Put one plant in the window and one in the dark, and measure how much they grow.
Step 5: Draw a Conclusion Look at your results. Did your hypothesis come true? Write down what you learned. Even if you were wrong, that's valuable information!
Step 6: Repeat! Try your experiment again to make sure you get the same results. Other people should be able to copy your experiment too.
