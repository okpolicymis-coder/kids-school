Part 1: Reading Comprehension

Why we're learning this: Last week you showed you can read carefully and understand scientific material — 4/5 correct! This week we're switching to fiction. When you read a story, good readers pay attention to four things: who the story is about (characters), where and when it happens (setting), what happens (plot), and what the story is really about (main idea/theme). These are called literary elements, and they're the building blocks of every story you'll ever read.

📖 Read this story, then answer the questions below:

The Kite That Wouldn't Fly

Leo had been waiting all week for Saturday. The weather report promised wind — perfect kite weather. He'd built his kite from scratch using wooden dowels, tissue paper, and string he'd saved from a birthday package. It was diamond-shaped with a long tail made from old ribbon.

On Saturday morning, Leo ran to the park with his kite tucked under his arm. The wind was blowing just right. He launched the kite into the air, but instead of soaring, it spun in circles and crashed into the grass. He tried again. Crash. Again. Crash.

Leo sat down on the bench, frustrated. An older man who had been walking his dog stopped and looked at the kite. "Mind if I take a look?" he asked. Leo nodded.

The man examined the kite carefully. "Your frame is solid," he said, "but your tail is too short. A kite's tail isn't just for looks — it keeps the kite stable in the wind. Without enough tail, the wind pushes it around."

Leo hadn't known that. He'd thought the tail was just decoration. The man helped him tie on extra ribbon from the spool Leo had brought. "Try now," the man said.

Leo launched the kite again. This time, it caught the wind and climbed — higher and higher until it was just a speck of color against the blue sky. Leo grinned. Sometimes the thing you think is just for show turns out to be the thing that keeps you steady.

Answer these questions about the story:

1. Who is the main character of this story?
2. Where and when does the story take place? (This is called the SETTING)
3. What was the PROBLEM Leo faced? (This is part of the PLOT)
4. How was the problem SOLVED?
5. What do you think is the MAIN IDEA or lesson of this story? (What did Leo learn?)

Part 2: Parts of Speech

Why we're learning this: Every word in a sentence has a job. The three most common jobs are:

👤 Nouns: A person, place, thing, or idea. Examples: Leo, park, kite, wind, Saturday.
🏃 Verbs: An action word or a state of being. Examples: ran, launched, is, was, grinned.
🎨 Adjectives: A word that describes a noun. Examples: wooden, diamond-shaped, frustrated, blue, steady.

Being able to identify these three parts of speech helps you understand how sentences are built — and it makes you a stronger writer because you can choose your words more carefully.
